## The short answer

Molecules in the atmosphere scatter shorter wavelengths of visible light more strongly than longer ones. Blue light is scattered much more readily than red, so blue reaches our eyes from across the sky.

## The light that changes direction

Sunlight contains a range of wavelengths. Some travels fairly directly toward the ground. Some interacts with the atmosphere and is redirected.

This scattering means you can receive sunlight even when you look away from the Sun. The atmosphere sends some of it toward you from other directions. Because the scattering depends strongly on wavelength, the resulting sky has a different mix of colors from the incoming sunlight.

## The same air, a longer path

When the Sun is low, its light travels through more atmosphere before reaching you. More of the shorter-wavelength light gets scattered out of the direct path, helping explain the warmer colors of sunrise and sunset.

The color overhead is a clue to the route light has taken. Looking up means seeing both sunlight and what happened to it on the way.
