## The short answer

An octopus has **three hearts**. Two branchial hearts push blood through the gills. A systemic heart sends oxygenated blood to the rest of the body.

## Two stages of the same trip

The hearts do different parts of the circulation. Blood needs to pick up oxygen at the gills before that oxygen can reach the tissues that use it. The two branchial hearts handle the gill stage; the systemic heart handles distribution around the body.

So three hearts doesn't mean three spare pumps doing the same thing. It means a division of work.

## Why blue blood?

Human blood uses an iron-containing molecule, hemoglobin, to carry oxygen. Cephalopods use a copper-containing molecule called hemocyanin. That chemistry accounts for their blue blood.

The familiar human arrangement is only one way to transport oxygen. An octopus does the same essential job with a different molecule and a different set of pumps. Knowing which heart sends blood where makes the unusual number easier to understand.
