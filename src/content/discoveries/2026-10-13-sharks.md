## The short answer

The lineage that includes sharks reaches back before trees appeared. But the earliest evidence includes shark-like animals whose exact relationships scientists still debate.

## A family, not a single survivor

Calling sharks older than trees can make it sound as if a modern great white has been swimming around unchanged since then. That isn't what the fossil record shows.

The Natural History Museum describes a long history of different forms. Some animals once grouped with sharks may be closer to chimaeras, another branch of cartilaginous fishes. Teeth and fragments provide clues, but deciding where an ancient animal belongs takes more than noticing a resemblance.

## Why the wording matters

An old lineage can contain species that appeared much later. A family history is different from the age of one family member.

The more careful version of the fact is still striking: the seas held early relatives of sharks before forests existed on land. Modern sharks are part of that history, with plenty of evolutionary change between then and now.
