## The short answer

The second is defined using a particular transition in the cesium-133 atom. The frequency associated with that transition is exactly **9,192,631,770 cycles per second**.

## A steadier reference

The rotation of Earth offers an intuitive clock: one turn, one day. But Earth's rotation isn't perfectly uniform. Atomic clocks provide a more stable reference for precise timekeeping.

They don't watch individual atoms swing like pendulums. Instead, they use a repeatable atomic transition as the reference for a frequency. That lets laboratories realize the same unit of time independently.

## Two clocks to keep in mind

Time based on Earth's rotation and time based on atomic clocks can drift apart. Leap seconds have been used to keep Coordinated Universal Time close to astronomical time.

This is why measuring a second and following the Sun are related but different tasks. The atom gives us a stable unit. Earth gives us the cycle of daylight that our daily lives are built around.
